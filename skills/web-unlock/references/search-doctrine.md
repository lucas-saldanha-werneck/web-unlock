# Search doctrine: before saying "it does not exist"

Read this before you conclude that something does not exist, or before you publish a price list or a
comparison table built from searches. "I did not find it" is a fact about your search. "It does not exist"
is a claim about the world. Only the second one needs this checklist.

- **Coverage carries the conclusion.** To say "it does not exist", the floor is a general web search **and** a
  sweep of the suppliers in that field. A niche may need more. One marketplace is not the market: tooling,
  industrial parts and niche products are often sold through distributors and specialist shops.
- **At least one query by code and one by description, then vary until nothing new shows up.** A part number
  or model code (`HV-4`, `MR-222`, `DM5042`) is precise and finds catalogs. A description finds informal
  listings that only have free text. They cover each other; neither replaces the other. A code that worked on
  one channel goes to every channel.
- **A link in hand is cheaper than a new tool: open ALL of them before switching channels.** If a search result
  already named the right shop, open that link before you try another tool.
- **Evidence against your conclusion is a stop sign**, not noise to filter out. One contrary data point means
  you check again before publishing.
- **A catalog question needs enumeration, not search.** "What exists in category X" means `map` or `crawl` on
  the supplier's site, not `search`.
- **Check your tooling before concluding a negative.** Read the `--help` of what is installed and what your
  plugins and MCP servers offer. `map`, `crawl` and `search` solve what `scrape` alone does not.
- **When you scrape a listing, validate the pairing.** A title and the price next to it can be misaligned.
  Anchor on the real offset of each card and cross-check with a second source before publishing a table.
  Watch out: a product page sometimes renders its own price with JavaScript and gives the scraper only the
  prices of "related products".

When the user asks for a "deep" or "exhaustive" search, end with two short lists: where you looked, and
where you did not look and why.
